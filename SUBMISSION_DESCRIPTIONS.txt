---------------------------- WEEK 1: Agribusiness Data Landscape Exploration & Planning ----------------------------
This report explores the landscape of publicly available agribusiness data and sets out a strategic plan for the analysis work in later weeks. It begins with the main challenges in the sector, including climate variability, price volatility, fragmented supply chains, post-harvest loss, rising input costs, unequal water access and poor data quality, and turns each into a data question. Eight public sources are researched and described by publisher, content, granularity, format and access method: Agmarknet, data.gov.in, IMD, NASA POWER, ICRISAT, FAOSTAT, World Bank commodity price data and the Soil Health Card portal. Their strengths, limitations and best uses are compared, and an integration design shows how they can be joined. The report then reviews the main data types (crop yields, weather patterns, market prices, supply-chain metrics, soil and inputs, and farm practices), including typical variables, units, quality issues and derived metrics. A decision-use table links each type of data to business decisions such as procurement, storage and selling, fertiliser advice, logistics and contracts. The planned analysis functions are set out with data types, metrics and business impact, followed by a table showing how each approach addresses a common agribusiness challenge. A phased strategic plan for Weeks 2 to 5, a KPI framework covering agronomic, market, supply-chain, financial and data-quality indicators, a five-week timeline, a risk register and ethics and licensing considerations complete the document.

---------------------------- WEEK 2: Data Cleaning and Preparation ----------------------------
This report documents the data cleaning and preparation process for an aggregated, simulated agribusiness dataset that imitates data merged from several public sources. The raw file contains 1,545 farm-season records for four crops in eight districts and was built with realistic problems: duplicates, missing values, inconsistent category labels, mixed units, text-formatted prices, impossible values and outliers. The report profiles each problem with counts and scores baseline data quality for completeness, validity, uniqueness and format consistency. It then presents a step-by-step cleaning plan with a rationale for the order and purpose of every step, supported by pseudo-code snippets. Techniques are compared and justified, including a hide-and-recover experiment that tests four imputation methods, three outlier-detection approaches (valid ranges, IQR within crop and a cross-field plausibility rule) and several normalisation methods, with crop-wise yield indexing used for comparability. A challenges section explains how the order of operations, hidden missing data and mislabelled crop records were handled; the mislabelled records were found only through later model diagnostics, which led to an extra rule and a pipeline re-run. Integrity checks, a reconciled cleaning log and a before-and-after quality scorecard show that 1,545 records became 1,425 analysis-ready records. The report closes with nine recommendations for continuous quality improvement, including source validation, automated checks, master data, monitoring and data ownership.

---------------------------- WEEK 3: Exploratory Data Analysis and Visualization ----------------------------
This report presents an exploratory data analysis (EDA) of a simulated agribusiness dataset of 1,425 cleaned farm-season records covering crop performance, weather patterns and pricing trends. It defines the variables, sets out an EDA plan and then works through summary statistics (mean, median, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, skewness and kurtosis), distributions, correlation analysis using both Pearson and Spearman measures, and group comparisons by irrigation type, crop, season and district. Eleven embedded visualizations include a histogram grid, box plots, scatter plots with binned means, a correlation heat map, yearly trend lines, a price seasonality chart and a district-by-crop heat map, and each is accompanied by an interpretation. Key findings are that fertiliser and rainfall show the strongest associations with yield, rainfed farms lag irrigated farms, prices rise about 3.4 percent a year with a Rabi-season premium, and ragi and tomato prices are the most volatile. The report also identifies anomalies, including a seasonal yield gap that overlaps with rainfall, pooled correlations that exaggerate within-crop relationships, and mislabelled crop records. A table links each trend to a business implication and action. A further section discusses the challenges of finding meaningful trends (mixed crop scales, confounding, short series and skewed variables) and how they were addressed, followed by nine recommendations for further analysis and the visualization principles applied.

---------------------------- WEEK 4: Statistical Analysis and Predictive Modeling ----------------------------
This report applies statistical analysis and predictive modelling to hypothetical agribusiness data to understand and predict crop yield. The target is a crop-normalised yield index, and the predictors are rainfall, temperature, fertiliser rate (used as an input-cost proxy), soil pH, farm area, irrigation type and season. The methodology section gives a theoretical walkthrough of data processing from ingestion to monitoring, states the regression specification and explains why regression was chosen. Five hypotheses are stated with null and alternative forms and tested using Pearson correlation, ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis, Tukey post-hoc comparisons and a Welch t-test. An ordinary least squares regression explains about 70 percent of yield variation, with effects reported with 95 percent confidence intervals and standardised coefficients. Fertiliser, rainfall and irrigation type are significant and practically important, while temperature, soil pH, farm area and season are not. Model assumptions (linearity, independence, constant variance, normality, multicollinearity and influence) are each tested and reported, including charts, with a discussion of how a mild departure from normality was handled using robust standard errors. Predictive performance is validated using a train-test split and cross-validation against a baseline, a decision tree and a random forest, with error checked by group and by crop. A second regression models price outcomes. A detailed limitations section covers causation, clustering, omitted variables, extrapolation and multiple testing, and the report closes with conclusions and business implications.

---------------------------- WEEK 5: Business Insights, Reporting and Strategic Recommendations ----------------------------
This report is a professional business report that converts the analytical work of Weeks 1 to 4 into strategic recommendations for a hypothetical agribusiness, GreenHarvest Agri Co-operative in Karnataka. It starts with an executive summary and the business context, then gives a methodological overview covering landscape planning, data cleaning, exploratory analysis and statistical modelling. The key findings section consolidates data quality results, price and revenue trends, statistical tests, regression and predictive model performance (about 72 percent of yield variation explained on unseen data) and highlights from exploration and diagnostics. A dedicated section links each finding to a real-world agribusiness challenge, such as rainfall risk, water inequality, input efficiency, price seasonality, transport cost and data reliability. Eight strategic recommendations are grouped under operational efficiency, market competitiveness and business performance. Each recommendation explains the reason, the method of implementation, the potential benefits, the potential pitfalls with mitigations and a feasibility rating. The report adds a prioritisation matrix, an illustrative financial scenario for irrigation upgrades with honest caveats, a 12-month roadmap, KPIs with targets, and a cross-cutting risk table. It concludes with nine suggestions for further research, including causal evaluation, better weather features, price forecasting and cost-margin analysis, and an appendix mapping each recommendation to its supporting evidence and assumptions.
